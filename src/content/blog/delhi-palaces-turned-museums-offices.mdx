An art gallery, a railway headquarters, a court complex. These might sound like ordinary addresses in Delhi. Yet behind some of their gates stand buildings created for maharajas and princely rulers.

Around India Gate, names such as **Jaipur House, Bikaner House and Baroda House** preserve the identities of the royal families who established residences here. Their buildings have since taken on new roles, becoming part of the capital’s cultural and working life.

Once you know their stories, a familiar journey through central Delhi begins to look different.

## Why Are There So Many Royal Residences Around India Gate?

When the British announced the transfer of their capital from Calcutta to Delhi in 1911, the planning of New Delhi included a prestigious area for princely residences. Known as **Princes’ Park**, it surrounded what is now the India Gate hexagon.

Rulers from different princely states established impressive Delhi addresses here. These residences placed them close to the ceremonial and political centre of British India. Their names remain on the map, even as their occupants and purposes have changed. The [official history of Bikaner House](https://bikanerhouse.rajasthan.gov.in/history) explains how this princely neighbourhood took shape.

## 1. Jaipur House: A Royal Residence That Opened Its Doors to Art

Near India Gate, Jaipur House was built in the 1930s as a residence for the Maharaja of Jaipur. Its central dome and symmetrical wings give the building a distinctive presence.

In **1954**, it became home to the **National Gallery of Modern Art**. A residence associated with one royal family became a national institution dedicated to collecting and presenting Indian art. The [Museums of India portal](https://museumsofindia.gov.in/repository/museum/ngma_del) traces the building’s origins and the gallery’s inauguration.

That history adds another dimension to a museum visit. Alongside the paintings and sculptures, there is the building itself to notice: its proportions, its entrances and the way its rooms connect.

For visitors interested in both architecture and culture, Jaipur House offers an approachable introduction to Delhi’s princely past. You can come for the art and leave with a new understanding of the address.

## 2. Bikaner House: From Princely Hospitality to Contemporary Culture

Bikaner House opened in **1929** as the Delhi residence of the rulers of Bikaner. Built during Maharaja Ganga Singh’s reign, it combines Western architectural influences with Rajput details, including its domed *chhatris*.

After independence, the Government of Rajasthan acquired the property and used it for administrative purposes. Restoration under conservation architect Abha Narain Lambah later helped bring the building into a new phase, and it [reopened to the public in 2015](https://bikanerhouse.rajasthan.gov.in/history).

Today, exhibitions and cultural programmes give visitors reasons to spend time in its restored spaces. The building is an example of how conservation can make a historic property useful and inviting again.

Check the exhibition programme before visiting, then leave time to look beyond the artworks. The former residence is part of the experience.

## 3. Baroda House: A Palace at the Heart of the Railways

Baroda House offers one of the most unexpected transformations.

Designed by **Edwin Lutyens for the Maharaja of Baroda**, the residence reflects the ruler’s preference for a largely European architectural character. Its design includes courtyards, arcaded verandahs and a prominent entrance porch, described in [INTACH Delhi’s architectural account](https://artsandculture.google.com/story/fgVRBVx3eRsA8A?hl=en).

Today, it houses [Northern Railway’s headquarters](https://nr.indianrailways.gov.in/view_section.jsp?id=0,7&lang=0).

The railway sign gives the address a practical, everyday identity. Knowing its origins reveals another layer: a princely residence adapted to serve a major public institution.

Baroda House remains a working office, so visitors should not assume access to its interiors. Its history nevertheless makes it a compelling stop in the story of central Delhi.

## 4. Patiala House: A Royal Address Becomes a Place of Justice

For many people, the name Patiala House immediately brings court proceedings to mind. Its earlier life belonged to the royal family of Patiala.

The site was allotted to **Maharaja Bhupinder Singh**, and the palace was constructed by his son, **Maharaja Yadavindra Singh**. It later accommodated the Delhi High Court before becoming associated with the district courts. This history is discussed in the [Delhi Lieutenant Governor’s address on *Patiala House: From Palace to Seat of Justice*](https://lg.delhi.gov.in/media/speeches/address-honble-lt-governor-launch-patiala-house-palace-seat-justice-justice-poonam).

Its changing use offers a striking way to think about Delhi’s history. A residence built for a hereditary ruler became a setting for the institutions of a democratic republic.

As an active court complex, it should be approached as a place of work. Its royal origins are a story to appreciate without treating the premises as a sightseeing venue.

## 5. Hyderabad House: Royal Reception Rooms Find a Diplomatic Role

Designed by **Edwin Lutyens for Osman Ali Khan, the Nizam of Hyderabad**, Hyderabad House was among the grandest princely residences in New Delhi. Its central dome, spreading wings and richly detailed interiors were designed for ceremonial hospitality.

That purpose has continued in a different form. Under the Ministry of External Affairs, the building hosts visiting foreign leaders, official meetings and state banquets. The [India Tourism Development Corporation](https://itdc.co.in/hyderabad-house/) has handled its catering and upkeep services since 1974.

You may recognise the name from news reports without realising that the diplomatic venue began as a royal residence. It remains an official government setting, with restricted access.

## Exploring Delhi’s Former Royal Residences

For an afternoon exploring this history, begin with **Jaipur House and Bikaner House**, where art provides a welcoming way into the buildings. Check the [NGMA website](https://ngmaindia.gov.in/) and [Bikaner House’s programme](https://bikanerhouse.rajasthan.gov.in/) for current opening arrangements and exhibitions before setting out.

Then, the next time you pass a familiar government address near India Gate, look a little more closely. Its name may be the surviving clue to a royal household—and a very different Delhi.
