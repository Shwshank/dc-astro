# Your First 24 Hours in Delhi: A Practical Arrival Guide

Your first day in Delhi can involve too many decisions at once: transport, mobile data, money, food, traffic, and an unfamiliar neighbourhood. The solution is not a packed itinerary. It is a clear order of priorities.

<p class="highlight-bar">On Day One, get connected, get settled, and learn your immediate surroundings.</p>

Here is a practical plan from before landing to the following morning.

---

## Before Landing: Prepare These Five Things

Complete these before your flight or while you still have reliable internet:

1. **Save your accommodation offline.** Keep its full address, phone number, booking confirmation, and map location in screenshots.
2. **Choose your airport transport.** Arrange a pickup or decide which official cab or app-based option you will use.
3. **Download an offline map of Delhi.** Mark your accommodation and the airport terminal.
4. **Arrange mobile data.** Install an eSIM in advance or check what identification is currently required for a local SIM.
5. **Keep payment backups separate.** Carry two cards and a small amount of cash, but do not store everything in the same wallet.

Also send your flight and accommodation details to someone you trust. If a driver is meeting you, confirm the pickup point, name, phone number, and vehicle details.

## At the Airport: Ignore Unsolicited Transport Offers

After collecting your baggage, connect to mobile data or airport Wi-Fi and contact your driver. If you have not arranged a pickup, use a recognized ride-booking app or an official airport transport counter.

Before entering the vehicle:

- Match the registration number with your booking.
- Confirm the driver’s name and destination.
- Check that the ride has started in the app, if applicable.
- Keep your accommodation address open on your phone.
- Share the ride details with someone you trust.
- Keep your passport, phone, and wallet with you—not in checked luggage or an inaccessible bag.

Do not follow someone who claims your hotel is closed, your booking is invalid, or your chosen transport is unavailable. Call the accommodation directly using the number on your confirmed booking.

For more situations to watch for after landing, read [Common Tourist Scams in Delhi](/blog/common-tourist-scams-delhi).

## At Your Accommodation: Set Up a Reliable Base

Before sightseeing, make the room or apartment work for you:

- Confirm the Wi-Fi connection.
- Charge your phone and power bank.
- Secure your passport, spare card, and extra cash.
- Ask reception or your host for the nearest pharmacy and convenience store.
- Buy sealed bottled water or confirm that the available water is properly filtered.
- Save the accommodation entrance as a pin; large properties may have multiple gates.

If you are staying independently, check how you will re-enter the building at night and whom to call if the gate is locked.

## Mobile Data, Cash, and Payments

Mobile data is essential for maps, cab bookings, translation, and contacting your accommodation. An eSIM is usually the simplest way to have data immediately. For a longer stay, a local SIM may be more economical, but activation can require identification and verification.

Delhi uses cash, cards, and UPI. UPI is widespread, but it may not be immediately available to every international visitor. Keep a modest amount of cash for small shops, tips, or a failed card payment.

Practical payment habits:

- Confirm the amount on the terminal before tapping or entering your PIN.
- Use ATMs attached to banks or inside secure commercial spaces.
- Decline help from strangers while using an ATM.
- Keep your backup card separately from your main wallet.
- Do not exchange a large amount of money through an unsolicited individual.

## Your First Meal: Choose Freshly Cooked Food

After a flight, choose a dependable restaurant close to your accommodation. Order freshly cooked food, moderate the spice level, and drink sealed bottled or properly filtered water.

For the first meal, avoid:

- Food that has been sitting uncovered
- Raw garnishes if you are unsure how they were washed
- Ice when the water source is unclear
- An ambitious street-food crawl while tired or dehydrated

Street food is worth exploring later, when you have the energy to choose vendors carefully. Read the [Delhi Street Food Safety Guide](/blog/delhi-street-food-safety-guide) before you go.

## Rest Before Making an Evening Plan

After checking in, take a shower, eat, hydrate, and rest. Set an alarm if you nap so that jet lag does not turn a short break into an all-night sleep.

Before going out, check:

- The current weather and air quality
- Travel time in both directions
- The site’s closing time
- Whether your clothing is appropriate for a religious place
- How you will return after dark

If you still feel exhausted, skip the outing. Delhi will make considerably more sense after sleep.

## Choose One First Outing

Your first outing should be close, easy to leave, and limited to one area. Good options include:

- A walk around your immediate neighbourhood in daylight
- A garden or spacious monument
- A café followed by a short walk
- An early dinner close to your accommodation
- A drive through central Delhi without adding several stops

Avoid combining Old Delhi, multiple monuments, shopping, and nightlife on your arrival day. Places that appear close on a map can take much longer to reach in traffic.

When you are ready to travel farther, use the [Delhi Metro vs Cab vs Auto guide](/blog/delhi-metro-vs-cab-vs-auto) to choose the right transport for each journey.

<p class="highlight-bar">One well-chosen experience is more useful than five rushed stops.</p>

## Before Sleeping: Prepare Tomorrow

Take ten minutes to remove friction from the next morning:

- Pin your destination and accommodation on the map.
- Check opening hours and weekly closing days.
- Book transport if you have an early start.
- Charge your phone and power bank.
- Refill your water supply.
- Keep one card, some cash, and identification ready.
- Allow extra time between places instead of building a minute-by-minute itinerary.

## First-24-Hours Checklist

- [ ] Accommodation details saved offline
- [ ] Airport transport confirmed
- [ ] Working mobile data
- [ ] Small amount of Indian currency
- [ ] Backup card stored separately
- [ ] Drinking water available
- [ ] Passport and valuables secured
- [ ] One simple, freshly cooked meal
- [ ] One nearby outing at most
- [ ] Next morning’s route checked

Delhi does not need to be simplified, but your arrival can be. Handle the practical essentials first, reduce unnecessary decisions, and let the city appear in manageable layers.
