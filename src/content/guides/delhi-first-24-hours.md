Your first 24 hours in Delhi define your entire experience.

Most people make one mistake:
They try to do too much, too fast.

This creates sensory overload — noise, movement, attention, decisions — all at once.

This guide is about **controlled entry**.

---

## Phase 1: Arrival (0–3 Hours)

Your only goal:
**Stability, not exploration**

Focus on:
- Reaching your accommodation
- Hydrating
- Settling your immediate environment

Avoid:
- Street interactions
- Spontaneous plans
- Exploring nearby areas

---

## Phase 2: Orientation (3–12 Hours)

Now begin controlled exposure.

Do:
- A short walk (10–15 minutes max)
- Observe surroundings without engagement
- Identify:
  - nearest café
  - nearest pharmacy
  - nearest main road

Do NOT:
- Enter crowded markets
- Engage with aggressive sellers
- Try to “experience Delhi” yet

---

## Phase 3: Light Movement (12–24 Hours)

Now introduce structure.

Choose:
- One place
- One route
- One transport method

Example:
Hotel → Metro → India Gate → Return

Keep it simple.

---

## Sensory Overload Signals

Watch for:
- Mental fatigue
- Irritation without reason
- Decision paralysis

These are not problems.
They are signals.

When this happens:
→ Stop movement  
→ Return to base  

---

## Cognitive Relief Framework

Reduce variables:

- Pre-decide your route
- Limit interactions
- Keep duration short

Delhi becomes manageable when decisions are minimized.

---

## What Most Guides Get Wrong

They push:
- Markets
- Food trails
- “Must-see” places

Too early.

This creates exhaustion, not experience.

---

## Final Thought

Delhi is not meant to be consumed immediately.

It is meant to be entered gradually.

If your first 24 hours are calm,
the rest of the city opens naturally.
